Platform Choice

Why Shopify for Your Online Store

Shopify is a hosted platform. You don't manage servers, security patches, or hosting yourself. For a growing textile or retail brand, that matters, since it means you can focus on selling, not on technical maintenance you never signed up for.

Social commerce, selling through Instagram, Facebook, and WhatsApp, is expected to keep growing as a share of Pakistan's online retail, according to PCMI market research. A proper Shopify store gives customers who already know your brand from social media a real way to browse, checkout, and come back, instead of scrolling through old posts to find a product they liked last week.

The Honest Take

I recommend Shopify when a business is mainly about selling products, with a clear path from browsing to checkout. If you need a lot of custom content pages, a blog, or service based lead generation alongside a small store, WordPress with WooCommerce sometimes fits better instead. I build both, so I can tell you honestly which one actually matches your business.

Tech Stack

Shopify Tools and Technologies I Use

I build on Shopify's Online Store 2.0, using Dawn or a similar lightweight base theme for most projects, since it loads faster than older, bloated theme frameworks. For brands that need a distinct look and feel, I customize deeper with Liquid rather than forcing everything through a generic template.

For functionality, I work with Shopify's app ecosystem for reviews, upsells, and analytics, while keeping the app count low — every added app is one more script that can slow a store down. Beyond Shopify itself, I'm comfortable with HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and Shopify's REST and GraphQL APIs when a project needs custom functionality no app provides.
